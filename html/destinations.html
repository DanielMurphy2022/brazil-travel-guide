Destinations

The six, in detail

Rio de Janeiro

Fly into
Galeão (GIG), or Santos Dumont (SDU) from within Brazil
Best months
May to October, drier and cooler
World Heritage
Listed 2012

Rio is built into the gaps between granite peaks and the Atlantic, which is why it looks the way it does. The city is unusual in having a genuine rainforest inside it: Tijuca, replanted by hand in the nineteenth century after the hillsides had been cleared for coffee.

The whole landscape, rather than a single monument, is what UNESCO listed in 2012 under the title “Carioca Landscapes between the Mountain and the Sea”.

Iguaçu Falls

Fly into
Foz do Iguaçu (IGU)
Best months
Open all year; the falls carry most water from December to March
World Heritage
Listed 1986

Around 275 separate falls spread along nearly three kilometres of cliff, on the border between Paraná and Argentina. The Brazilian side gives you the panorama; the Argentine side puts you on walkways over the water. Most visitors do both, which means crossing the border.

Iguaçu National Park was inscribed as a natural World Heritage Site in 1986.

Salvador

Fly into
Salvador (SSA)
Best months
September to March, the drier half of the year
World Heritage
Listed 1985

Salvador was the capital of Brazil until 1763 and remains the centre of Afro-Brazilian culture. Capoeira, candomblé and the drumming that fed into modern Brazilian music all run through here.

The Pelourinho, its steep old town of pastel colonial houses, has been a World Heritage Site since 1985.

The Amazon

Fly into
Manaus (MAO)
Best months
High water February to June for boat trips; low water August to November for river beaches and forest walks
World Heritage
Listed 2000

Most trips start in Manaus, a city of two million reachable in practice only by plane or boat. Just downstream, the black water of the Rio Negro runs alongside the pale Solimões for several kilometres without mixing. This is the Meeting of the Waters.

The Central Amazon Conservation Complex was listed in 2000.

Ouro Preto

Fly into
Belo Horizonte (CNF), then about two hours by road
Best months
April to September, outside the summer rain
World Heritage
Listed 1980, the first site in Brazil

A gold-rush town in the hills of Minas Gerais that was, briefly in the 1700s, one of the richest places in the world. The money went into baroque churches, many carved by Aleijadinho, and then the gold ran out and the town stopped changing.

It was Brazil’s very first World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1980.

Lençóis Maranhenses

Fly into
São Luís (SLZ), then on by road to Barreirinhas
Best months
June to September, while the lagoons still hold rainwater
World Heritage
Listed 2024

A field of white dunes in Maranhão that fills with rainwater every wet season, leaving thousands of freshwater lagoons sitting between the sand ridges. They are at their fullest in the middle of the year and dry out again later, so timing matters more here than anywhere else in this guide.

The national park was added to the World Heritage List in 2024.
