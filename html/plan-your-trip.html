Plan your trip

The practical things that are easier to sort out before you book.

Getting there from Ireland

There is no direct flight from Dublin to Brazil, so every route involves at least one connection, most commonly through Lisbon, Madrid, Paris, Amsterdam or London. Counting the stop, budget the better part of a day each way.

São Paulo (GRU) is the busiest arrival point and usually the cheapest to reach; Rio (GIG) is the more obvious start if you only have a week. Internal distances are big enough that a domestic flight is often the only sensible way to link two regions.

The seasons run backwards

Brazil is mostly in the southern hemisphere, so summer falls roughly between December and March and winter between June and September. Irish school holidays in July therefore land in the Brazilian winter, which in much of the country still means pleasant weather, and in the north east barely registers at all.

Two things worth timing around: Carnival, which moves with Easter and fills hotels months ahead, and the Lençóis Maranhenses lagoons, which are only full for part of the year.

When to go

A rough guide to what each part of the year feels like on the ground.

Brazil spans several climate zones, so this is a general guide rather than a forecast. The far south and the north east can behave very differently in the same month.
Months Season What it means for a visitor
December to March Summer Hottest and busiest, and the wettest months across much of the country. Carnival falls in this window, so book far ahead.
April to June Autumn Quieter and still warm. The Amazon is at high water, which is the season for boat trips into the flooded forest.
July to September Winter Cool in the south, dry and pleasant in the north east. The Lençóis Maranhenses lagoons are at their fullest.
October to November Spring Fewer visitors and lower prices, with the rain starting to build again in the south east.

Money, power and time

The small details that catch people out on arrival.

Money

  • Currency Brazilian real, written R$, code BRL
  • Cards Widely accepted, including in small businesses
  • Pix The instant bank transfer system almost everyone uses; it needs a Brazilian account, so visitors fall back on cards

Power

  • Sockets Type N, the three-round-pin Brazilian standard
  • Voltage Both 127 V and 220 V are in use, and it varies by city
  • What to bring A Type N adaptor; check any appliance is rated for both voltages

Time

  • Main zone Brasília time, UTC−3, covering Rio, São Paulo and the coast
  • Versus Ireland Three hours behind in the Irish winter, four in the Irish summer
  • Clock changes Brazil no longer operates daylight saving time

Before you book

Entry rules, vaccination requirements and travel advice all change, and a guide like this goes out of date faster than the official sources do. Check the current position rather than taking any page’s word for it:

  • Entry and visa requirements for Irish passport holders, and the current security advice, are published by the Department of Foreign Affairs: Irish travel advice for Brazil (opens in a new tab).
  • Yellow fever vaccination is recommended for parts of Brazil and some countries ask for proof of it if you arrive from Brazil. Ask a travel clinic well ahead: the certificate only takes effect ten days after the injection.
  • Take out travel insurance that covers the activities you actually plan to do. Diving, hiking and internal flights are the usual gaps.

Staying sensible

Brazil rewards the same habits as any large city anywhere: keep your phone out of sight on the street, use apps rather than hailing taxis, and ask at your accommodation which areas are worth avoiding after dark. Locals will tell you straight if you ask.

Tap water is treated in the major cities but most people still drink bottled or filtered water, and it is what you will be served.

A suggested first trip

Two weeks, two regions, one internal flight. It sounds modest, and it is far more enjoyable than trying to see the whole country.

Days 1–6: Rio de Janeiro

Long enough to see Sugarloaf and Corcovado, spend real time on the beaches, walk Santa Teresa and take a day trip out to Paraty on the coast road.

Days 7–14: Salvador and the north east

A short flight north for the old town, the music and considerably warmer water, using Salvador as a base for the coast either side of it.
